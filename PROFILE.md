## First 30 days

The scheduled posts provide consistency; useful conversations build recognition. There is no guaranteed follower, reply or like count.

1. Spend 15–20 minutes daily reading and personally replying to a few relevant builders. Add a concrete observation or a real question. Never copy the same reply across accounts.
2. Keep the ten-slot automation as a ceiling. Review published posts daily and correct mistakes openly. If it becomes repetitive, reduce volume rather than adding sensational hooks.
3. Each week, manually share one small experiment, comparison or tutorial you actually performed. Say what you tried, what failed and what someone else can reproduce. Do not let AI invent firsthand experience.
4. Reply personally when someone asks a question. Add a short self-reply with a source or clarification only when it adds value. Automated reply bots are not enabled.
5. Track weekly: posts published, impressions where visible, profile visits where visible, new followers, substantive replies and the topics that triggered them. Compare median results, not just the biggest outlier. Use only analytics available to your account.
6. After two weeks, keep the two topics that generate the most useful conversations. Test one change at a time: opener, subject or posting window. Global English and distributed UTC slots are a starting point, not evidence of an optimal schedule.

## Pseudonymity

Use a separate handle/avatar/email and avoid copying biographies from personal accounts. Do not publish exact location, employer, personal screenshots or identifying links. Review discoverability/contact-upload settings. A pseudonym reduces public linkage; it does not make the account untraceable. This repository already has a personally identifying owner name.

## Voice

Short, specific, curious, and playfully irreverent. Use a short hook, one blank line, then the concrete takeaway. Occasional "what the fuck?", "ohhhhh myyy, what the hell??" or "damn itttt" is welcome when earned; vary it with calmer observations. Direct swearing at the situation, never a person. A reaction must earn its place by explaining what changed. Prefer "Wait, what? The review step is now the workflow." over a context-free shock hook. Avoid pretending a product claim is independently tested, company affiliation, recycled outrage, engagement bait and excessive emoji.
